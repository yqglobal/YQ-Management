# 3. What Are Cookies?

A **Cookie** is a small piece of information stored in a browser or on a device when a website or application is accessed.

Cookies can be:

### First-party

A first-party Cookie is placed by the website or application you are visiting.

### Third-party

A third-party Cookie or similar technology is placed or controlled by another service used in connection with the website or application.

Cookies can also be:

### Session Cookies

Session Cookies generally remain available while a browsing session is active and may be removed when the browser session ends.

### Persistent Cookies

Persistent Cookies remain on the device for a defined period or until manually removed.

Cookies can perform different functions, including:

* authentication;
* security;
* remembering preferences;
* maintaining functionality;
* analytics;
* advertising or marketing.

# 6. Strictly Necessary Cookies

Strictly Necessary Cookies are technologies required to provide a service or functionality that you have requested.

Examples may include cookies or similar technologies used to:

* maintain an authenticated session;
* protect account security;
* maintain security-related state;
* remember a cookie-consent decision;
* support core application functionality;
* maintain load balancing or service availability;
* prevent certain types of abuse.

Where a technology is genuinely necessary to provide the requested service, applicable law may permit it to operate without separate consent.

The fact that a technology is useful to Qmova does not automatically make it strictly necessary.

The classification depends on the actual purpose of the technology. Regulatory guidance distinguishes technologies essential to providing a requested service from technologies that are merely helpful or convenient.

# 7. Functional Cookies

Functional technologies allow Qmova to remember preferences or provide functionality beyond the minimum required to deliver the service.

Examples may include:

* interface preferences;
* selected language;
* display preferences;
* non-essential feature configuration.

Whether a particular functional technology requires consent depends on its purpose and the applicable jurisdiction.

Where consent is required, Qmova will ask before enabling it.

# 8. Analytics Cookies

Analytics technologies help Qmova understand how websites or applications are used.

Depending on the technology configured, analytics may help us understand:

* which pages are visited;
* how users navigate through the website;
* whether pages perform correctly;
* which features are used;
* how the service performs;
* where users encounter errors.

Analytics technologies are not automatically considered strictly necessary simply because they improve Qmova's understanding of the service.

Where applicable law requires consent for analytics technologies, Qmova will obtain consent before enabling them. Current regulatory guidance generally treats analytics technologies as non-essential where they are not necessary for the service requested by the user.

Qmova will not identify a particular analytics provider in this Policy unless that provider is actually deployed in the relevant Qmova environment.

# 11. Cookie Consent

Qmova provides a cookie-consent mechanism where required.

The consent interface may allow users to:

* accept optional categories;
* reject optional categories;
* customize preferences;
* change preferences later.

Qmova does not rely solely on continued browsing as consent for optional technologies where applicable law requires an affirmative choice.

Qmova's consent mechanism is designed to record the user's selected preferences so that those preferences can be respected.

# 13. Browser Controls

Most modern browsers allow users to:

* view stored Cookies;
* delete Cookies;
* block Cookies;
* block third-party Cookies;
* configure automatic deletion;
* browse using private or incognito modes.

Browser controls differ between browsers and operating systems.

Blocking all Cookies may prevent certain Qmova functionality from operating correctly, particularly authentication and other core application features.

Browser controls provide an additional mechanism for managing device storage, but they do not necessarily replace Qmova's consent mechanism where a legally required consent record must be maintained.
