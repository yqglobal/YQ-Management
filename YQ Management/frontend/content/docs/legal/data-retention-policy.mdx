# 1. At a Glance

Qmova does not intend to retain Personal Information indefinitely.

Our retention approach is based on a simple principle:

> **Keep information for as long as it is reasonably necessary for its purpose, then delete, anonymize, or otherwise dispose of it appropriately.**

In practice:

* Customer Data is generally retained to provide the Services and according to the Customer's contractual and operational requirements.
* Some security and audit information may need to be retained for longer periods to protect the Platform and investigate incidents.
* Legal, regulatory, accounting, and dispute-related requirements may require certain information to be retained.
* Deleting information from active production systems does not necessarily remove it immediately from backups.
* Backup copies follow their own lifecycle and are not normally restored or accessed except when necessary.
* Customers may export supported Customer Data before termination or deletion.
* Customers remain responsible for determining appropriate retention periods for information they collect through their Workspaces.
* Qmova periodically reviews retention practices to avoid unnecessary storage.

This policy describes Qmova's retention framework. Specific retention periods may vary by data category, Customer agreement, legal requirement, and system architecture.

# 4. Retention Principles

Qmova's retention practices are guided by the following principles.

## Purpose Limitation

Information should not be retained after the purpose for which it is needed has ended unless another lawful or legitimate purpose applies.

## Data Minimization

Qmova should avoid retaining more information than necessary.

## Legal Compliance

Information may need to be retained to comply with legal, regulatory, accounting, tax, or other applicable obligations.

## Security

Certain information may need to be retained to investigate security events, fraud, abuse, or unauthorized access.

## Accountability

Some information may need to be retained to demonstrate that important security, privacy, or contractual actions occurred.

## Customer Control

Customers remain responsible for determining appropriate retention for Customer Data within their own business context, subject to Qmova's technical capabilities and contractual terms.

## Defined Lifecycle

Information should have an identifiable lifecycle from creation through deletion, anonymization, or another appropriate disposition.

# 5. Data Ownership and Responsibility

Qmova distinguishes between information processed for Qmova's own purposes and Customer Data processed on behalf of a Customer.

## Qmova-Controlled Information

Qmova may determine retention periods for information used for:

* account administration;
* security;
* billing;
* support;
* legal compliance;
* platform administration.

## Customer Data

For Customer Data processed on behalf of a Customer, the Customer generally determines the purpose and applicable retention requirements.

Qmova processes Customer Data according to:

* the Customer's documented instructions;
* the Customer Agreement;
* the Data Processing Addendum;
* applicable law;
* technical capabilities of the Services.

# 7. Account Data

Account information may include:

* name;
* email address;
* authentication identifiers;
* Workspace memberships;
* account preferences;
* security settings.

Account information may be retained while the account remains active.

After account closure, Qmova may retain limited information where necessary for:

* legal compliance;
* security;
* fraud prevention;
* accounting;
* dispute resolution;
* recordkeeping.

Information no longer required for an identified purpose becomes eligible for deletion according to the applicable lifecycle.

# 8. Customer and Workspace Data

Customer Data stored within a Workspace is generally retained while:

* the Workspace is active;
* the Customer requires the information;
* Qmova is contractually required to retain it;
* another lawful purpose applies.

Customers should configure their use of Qmova so that information is not collected or retained beyond what is appropriate for their operational purposes.

Where a Customer requires a specific contractual retention period, the requirement should be documented in the applicable Customer Agreement or DPA.

# 19. Backups

Backups are part of Qmova's resilience architecture.

Deleting information from active systems does not necessarily delete every backup copy immediately.

A simplified lifecycle is:

```text id="n8zqfi"
Active Production Data
        │
        ▼
Deletion from Active System
        │
        ▼
Backup Copy Remains During
Applicable Backup Lifecycle
        │
        ▼
Backup Rotation / Expiration
        │
        ▼
Backup Copy No Longer Available
```

Backup systems are primarily maintained for:

* recovery;
* resilience;
* protection from accidental loss;
* certain security incidents.

Backups are not intended to function as an alternative user-accessible archive.

Qmova does not ordinarily restore deleted information merely because a backup copy exists.

# 22. Customer Subscription Termination

When a Customer's subscription or Customer Agreement ends, Customer Data is not necessarily deleted at the exact moment service access is disabled.

Depending on the applicable agreement, the lifecycle may be:

```text id="m5l6sw"
Subscription Ends
       │
       ▼
Access Restricted
       │
       ▼
Applicable Export Period
       │
       ▼
Deletion from Active Systems
       │
       ▼
Backup Lifecycle
       │
       ▼
Deletion Lifecycle Complete
```

The exact periods and process should be governed by:

* the Customer Agreement;
* the DPA;
* applicable retention requirements;
* Qmova's technical capabilities.

Enterprise Customers may have different contractual retention and deletion provisions.

# 24. Legal and Regulatory Retention

Qmova may retain information beyond an ordinary operational lifecycle where required by law or reasonably necessary to:

* comply with legal obligations;
* satisfy tax or accounting requirements;
* respond to lawful authority requests;
* establish or defend legal claims;
* preserve evidence;
* meet contractual obligations.

Where information is retained for a specific legal or dispute-related purpose, access and use should remain limited to that purpose where appropriate.

---

# 25. Security and Incident-Related Retention

Information related to a security incident may need to be retained beyond ordinary operational periods.

Examples include:

* relevant audit logs;
* authentication records;
* security alerts;
* incident timelines;
* forensic information;
* remediation records.

This information may be retained to:

* investigate the incident;
* identify root cause;
* meet legal obligations;
* demonstrate appropriate response;
* prevent recurrence.

Retention should remain proportionate to the incident and purpose.

---

# 26. Aggregated and De-identified Data

Qmova may create aggregated or de-identified information where permitted by applicable law.

Such information may be retained after the underlying Personal Information has been deleted where it is no longer reasonably capable of being used to identify an individual.

Potential uses may include:

* operational statistics;
* system-performance analysis;
* capacity planning;
* product improvement;
* security analysis.

Information that remains identifiable is not treated as permanently de-identified merely because it has been aggregated with other information.

---

# 27. Data Minimization and Review

Retention is not a "keep everything forever" policy.

Qmova may periodically review:

* data categories;
* storage locations;
* retention periods;
* security logs;
* application records;
* backup configurations.

The purpose of these reviews is to identify information that can be:

* deleted;
* anonymized;
* aggregated;
* reduced;
* moved to a more appropriate storage lifecycle.

Retention requirements should be reviewed when:

* a new feature is introduced;
* a data category changes;
* a legal requirement changes;
* a Subprocessor changes;
* the architecture changes.

---

# 28. Customer Responsibilities

Customers are responsible for managing their own retention decisions for Customer Data.

Customers should:

* collect only information needed for their purposes;
* establish appropriate internal retention policies;
* remove unnecessary information;
* restrict access to retained data;
* comply with applicable legal requirements;
* consider sector-specific retention rules;
* request exports before terminating a service where necessary.

Qmova does not determine how long a hospital, bank, university, government office, or other Customer is legally required to retain its operational records.

# 29. Data Retention Requests

Customers may contact Qmova regarding retention requirements or contractual deletion instructions.

Requests should be directed to:

**[privacy@qmova.com](mailto:privacy@qmova.com)**

Where the request concerns Customer Data processed on behalf of a Customer, Qmova may require confirmation that the requester is authorized by the Customer.

Where a Customer requires a retention period different from Qmova's standard operational lifecycle, the parties may address that requirement through an applicable commercial or DPA arrangement where technically and legally feasible.

---

# 30. Exceptions

There may be circumstances where information is retained longer than the ordinary lifecycle.

Examples include:

* litigation;
* legal investigations;
* regulatory obligations;
* active security investigations;
* fraud investigations;
* unresolved disputes;
* contractual obligations;
* technical recovery requirements.

When a legal hold or equivalent preservation requirement applies, relevant information should not be deleted until the requirement is released.

Retained information should continue to receive appropriate security and access controls.

---

# 31. International Considerations

Qmova serves Customers internationally.

Retention requirements may differ between jurisdictions.

For example, a Customer may be subject to:

* South African privacy requirements;
* GDPR requirements;
* sector-specific regulations;
* local recordkeeping laws;
* tax requirements;
* contractual retention obligations.

Where Customer instructions conflict with mandatory legal retention obligations applicable to Qmova, Qmova may be required to retain the information for the legally required period.

International processing and transfer requirements are addressed separately in the Qmova Privacy Policy and DPA.
