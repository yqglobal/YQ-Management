# 1. At a Glance

Qmova uses a specific set of terms to describe how the platform works.

The most important concept is the **Visit**.

A Visit represents an interaction between a Visitor and a Customer and can enter Qmova through different paths, including an Appointment, Walk-in, Queue, Referral, or Staff-created workflow.

This glossary establishes the canonical meaning of Qmova terminology.

The definitions in this document are intended to be used consistently throughout Qmova's:

* Product Documentation;
* API Documentation;
* Trust Center;
* Legal Center;
* Compliance Documentation;
* Help Center; and
* customer-facing interfaces.

Where a Qmova document uses a defined term with capitalization, it should generally be interpreted according to this glossary unless that document expressly provides a different definition.

---

# 2. Purpose

Qmova operates across multiple industries and workflows.

A hospital, bank, university, government office, salon, restaurant, or service center may use different language for similar operational concepts.

Qmova therefore uses a common vocabulary so that the platform can represent different business models without changing the underlying terminology.

This glossary establishes that vocabulary.

It also helps prevent different Qmova documents from using multiple terms for the same concept.

---

# 3. How to Use This Glossary

## 3.1 Canonical Terms

Terms shown in **bold** represent Qmova's preferred terminology.

Documentation contributors should use these terms instead of introducing synonyms when referring to the corresponding Qmova concept.

For example, use:

> **Workspace**

rather than creating a separate term such as "Organization Portal", "Account Environment", or "Client Instance".

---

## 3.2 Product Terms vs. Generic Terms

Some words have both a general meaning and a Qmova-specific meaning.

For example:

* "customer" can mean any customer in ordinary conversation;
* **Customer** refers specifically to an organization using Qmova as a Customer.

Capitalization is therefore meaningful in formal Qmova documentation.

---

## 3.3 Legal Definitions

The definitions in legal agreements may be more specific where necessary to create contractual obligations.

Where a contract expressly defines a term differently for legal purposes, the contractual definition governs that agreement.

This glossary is primarily intended to establish consistent product and documentation terminology.

# 4. Core Qmova Concepts

## 4.1 Visit

A **Visit** is the central operational record representing an interaction between a Visitor and a Customer through Qmova.

A Visit can originate from:

* an Appointment;
* a Walk-in;
* a Queue;
* a Referral; or
* a Staff-created Visit.

A Visit may contain information such as:

* Visitor information;
* Service;
* location;
* appointment details;
* Queue status;
* timestamps;
* assigned Staff Member;
* Visit status;
* relevant operational notes.

The precise information associated with a Visit depends on how the Customer configures its Workspace and the Qmova features it uses.

---

## 4.2 Visit Lifecycle

The **Visit Lifecycle** is the progression of a Visit from creation through its final operational state.

A simplified lifecycle may include:

```text
Created
   │
   ▼
Scheduled / Waiting
   │
   ▼
Checked In
   │
   ▼
Queued
   │
   ▼
In Service
   │
   ▼
Completed
```

Other states may apply depending on the workflow, including:

* Cancelled;
* No-show;
* Rescheduled;
* Rejected;
* Missed; or
* other configured states.

The exact lifecycle available to a Customer depends on Qmova functionality and Customer configuration.

---

## 4.3 Appointment

An **Appointment** is a Visit that has been scheduled for a particular time, date, Service, Staff Member, location, or other configured resource.

An Appointment may later become an active Visit in the Customer's operational workflow.

An Appointment does not necessarily mean that the Visitor will immediately receive service upon arrival. The Customer may operate a Queue, verification process, or other arrival workflow.

---

## 4.4 Walk-in

A **Walk-in** is a Visit initiated without a previously scheduled Appointment.

A Walk-in may enter a Queue or another Customer-configured workflow after arrival.

The Customer may collect information from the Visitor during the Walk-in creation or Check-in process.

---

## 4.5 Queue

A **Queue** is an ordered operational flow of waiting Visits.

A Queue may be used to manage Visitors waiting for:

* a Service;
* a Staff Member;
* a location;
* a resource; or
* another Customer-defined operational process.

Queue ordering may depend on Customer configuration and applicable business rules.

Qmova does not assume that every Queue must operate using simple first-in-first-out ordering.

---

## 4.6 QR Check-in

**QR Check-in** is a Qmova workflow that allows a Visitor to access a Customer's designated Check-in experience by scanning a QR code.

Depending on configuration, QR Check-in may:

* identify a location;
* identify a Service;
* initiate a Visit;
* connect an existing Appointment;
* place a Visitor into a Queue;
* provide a digital ticket or confirmation.

A QR Check-in flow does not by itself provide administrative access to a Workspace.

---

## 4.7 Check-in

**Check-in** is the process through which Qmova records that a Visitor has arrived or otherwise entered a Customer's Visit workflow.

Check-in may occur through:

* QR Check-in;
* Staff action;
* another Customer-configured workflow.

Check-in does not necessarily mean that service has begun.

---

## 4.8 Digital Ticket

A **Digital Ticket** is a digital representation of a Visitor's position or status within a Customer's operational workflow.

A Digital Ticket may contain information such as:

* ticket number;
* Queue position;
* Visit status;
* Service;
* estimated waiting time;
* instructions.

The exact information displayed depends on the Customer's configuration and supported Qmova functionality.

---

# 5. Account and Organization Concepts

## 5.1 Customer

A **Customer** is an organization or business that subscribes to or otherwise uses Qmova.

A Customer may operate one or more Workspaces depending on its Qmova configuration and commercial arrangement.

A Customer determines how it uses Qmova for its own operational purposes.

For privacy purposes, a Customer may act as a Data Controller for personal information it processes through Qmova, while Qmova generally acts as a Data Processor for that Customer Data.

---

## 5.2 Workspace

A **Workspace** is a Customer's operational environment within Qmova.

A Workspace may contain:

* locations;
* Services;
* Staff Members;
* Visits;
* Appointments;
* Queues;
* roles;
* permissions;
* integrations;
* configurations;
* analytics.

The Workspace is the primary customer-facing concept for the logical environment in which Customer operations take place.

---

## 5.3 Tenant

A **Tenant** describes the logical isolation model used by Qmova to separate one Customer environment from another within the multi-tenant platform.

In customer-facing documentation, Qmova generally prefers the term **Workspace**.

The term **Tenant** may be used in technical, architectural, security, or legal contexts where describing the underlying multi-tenant model is useful.

A Tenant and a Workspace generally refer to the same logical Customer environment, viewed from different perspectives.

---

## 5.4 Location

A **Location** represents a physical or operational site associated with a Customer's Workspace.

Examples may include:

* a hospital branch;
* a bank branch;
* a university office;
* a government service center;
* a salon;
* a restaurant.

A Location may contain or provide:

* Services;
* Staff Members;
* Queue workflows;
* Appointment availability;
* QR Check-in points.

A Customer may have one or multiple Locations.

---

## 5.5 Service

A **Service** is an offering, task, or operational category that a Customer provides through Qmova.

Examples include:

* medical consultation;
* passport application;
* account opening;
* haircut;
* customer support;
* document verification.

A Service can be associated with a Location, Staff Member, Appointment, Queue, or Visit depending on the Customer's configuration.

---

## 5.6 Staff Member

A **Staff Member** is an authorized individual associated with a Customer's Workspace who performs or manages operational activities.

A Staff Member may:

* manage Visits;
* serve Visitors;
* manage Appointments;
* manage Queues;
* update Visit status;
* access analytics;
* administer specific areas of a Workspace.

The exact capabilities of a Staff Member are determined by assigned roles and permissions.

---

## 5.7 Role

A **Role** is a named collection of permissions assigned to a user within a Workspace.

Examples may include administrative or operational roles.

Roles are used to simplify access management by grouping related permissions.

The exact roles available may vary as Qmova evolves.

---

## 5.8 Permission

A **Permission** is an authorization that allows a user or role to perform a particular action or access a particular resource.

Examples may include permission to:

* view Visits;
* manage Appointments;
* update Queue state;
* manage Staff Members;
* change Workspace settings;
* access analytics.

Permissions are enforced through the application's authorization mechanisms.

---

# 6. Visit Management Concepts

## 6.1 Visitor

A **Visitor** is an individual who receives, requests, or participates in a service or interaction managed through a Customer's Qmova Workspace.

A Visitor may interact with Qmova without creating a permanent user account.

A Visitor can therefore be different from a Qmova **User**.

For example, a Visitor may scan a QR Check-in code and join a Queue without registering for a Qmova account.

---

## 6.2 User

A **User** is an individual who has an authenticated Qmova account and can access Qmova functionality according to their Workspace memberships and permissions.

A User may also be a Staff Member, administrator, or other authorized participant.

Not every Visitor is a User.

---

## 6.3 Visit Status

A **Visit Status** describes the current operational state of a Visit.

Examples may include:

* scheduled;
* waiting;
* checked in;
* queued;
* called;
* in service;
* completed;
* cancelled;
* no-show.

The exact status model depends on the Customer's workflow and Qmova's supported functionality.

---

## 6.4 Wait Time

**Wait Time** is the amount of time a Visitor has waited or is expected to wait within a Customer's Visit workflow.

Depending on context, Qmova may distinguish between:

* actual wait time;
* estimated wait time;
* Queue wait time;
* service wait time.

Any estimated wait time should be understood as an operational estimate rather than a guarantee.

---

## 6.5 Estimated Wait Time

**Estimated Wait Time** is a calculated approximation of how long a Visitor may wait before reaching a relevant point in the Visit Lifecycle.

Estimates may depend on factors such as:

* Queue position;
* current processing speed;
* active Visits;
* Service configuration;
* appointment schedules;
* Customer-defined rules.

Estimated Wait Time can change as real-world operating conditions change.

---

## 6.6 No-Show

A **No-show** is a Visit or Appointment that is treated as missed because the Visitor did not arrive or complete the required arrival process within the applicable Customer workflow.

The specific conditions for a No-show are determined by the Customer's configuration and operational policies.

---

## 6.7 Cancellation

A **Cancellation** occurs when an Appointment or Visit is intentionally ended before completion according to the applicable workflow.

A Cancellation may be performed by:

* a Visitor;
* a Staff Member;
* a Customer administrator;
* an automated workflow.

---

## 6.8 Referral

A **Referral** is a Visit entry path in which an individual is directed to a Customer or Service through another person, organization, workflow, or system.

The exact meaning and data associated with a Referral depend on the Customer's implementation.

---

# 7. Scheduling and Queue Concepts

## 7.1 Scheduling

**Scheduling** is the process of allocating an Appointment or expected Visit to a particular time, Service, Staff Member, Location, capacity, or combination of these.

Qmova may support different scheduling models depending on product functionality.

---

## 7.2 Slot

A **Slot** is a period of time made available for an Appointment or other scheduled activity.

Slot duration and availability are determined by Customer configuration and supported scheduling functionality.

---

## 7.3 Queue Position

**Queue Position** represents a Visitor's relative position within a Queue at a particular point in time.

Queue position may change as Visits enter, leave, or move through the Queue.

A Queue Position is not necessarily a permanent numerical value.

---

## 7.4 Calling

**Calling** is the operational action of selecting or notifying a Visitor when their Visit is ready for the next stage of the workflow.

Calling may trigger:

* a display update;
* staff notification;
* Visitor notification;
* status change.

---

## 7.5 Service Time

**Service Time** is the period during which a Visitor is actively receiving a Service or being processed by a Staff Member.

Service Time may be measured for operational analytics and may influence certain Queue or scheduling calculations where supported.

---

# 8. Customer and Visitor Concepts

## 8.1 Customer Data

**Customer Data** means information submitted to Qmova by or on behalf of a Customer for processing in connection with the Customer's use of the platform.

Customer Data may include:

* Visitor information;
* Visits;
* Appointments;
* Queue records;
* Service information;
* Staff information;
* operational notes;
* notification records;
* configurations.

The exact scope depends on how the Customer uses Qmova.

---

## 8.2 Personal Information

**Personal Information** means information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, or equivalent information covered by applicable privacy laws.

Depending on applicable law, this concept may be referred to as:

* personal data;
* personal information;
* personally identifiable information; or
* another legally equivalent term.

Qmova generally uses **Personal Information** as its broad documentation term.

---

## 8.3 Sensitive Personal Information

**Sensitive Personal Information** means categories of information that receive additional protection under applicable privacy laws.

Depending on jurisdiction, this may include information such as:

* health information;
* biometric information;
* financial information;
* identification information;
* information relating to children;
* other specially protected categories.

Qmova Customers are responsible for determining whether the information they process through a Workspace is subject to additional legal or regulatory requirements.

---

# 9. Access and Security Concepts

## 9.1 Authentication

**Authentication** is the process of verifying the identity of a user attempting to access Qmova.

Qmova supports authentication mechanisms including Google OAuth, email authentication, and OTP-based authentication.

---

## 9.2 Authorization

**Authorization** is the process of determining what an authenticated User is permitted to access or do.

Authentication and authorization are separate security concepts.

---

## 9.3 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

**Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** is the mechanism used to assign permissions to Users through Roles.

RBAC helps Customers manage access without individually configuring every permission for every User.

---

## 9.4 Session

A **Session** represents an authenticated period during which a User is accessing Qmova.

Session information may be used to maintain authentication and manage active access.

Qmova may provide controls that allow Users to review or manage active sessions.

---

## 9.5 Audit Log

An **Audit Log** is a record of relevant administrative, security, or operational actions.

Depending on the event, an Audit Log may include:

* actor;
* action;
* timestamp;
* affected resource;
* Workspace context;
* other information necessary to understand the event.

---

## 9.6 Security Event

A **Security Event** is an event that may be relevant to the security, integrity, availability, or confidentiality of Qmova systems or information.

Examples may include:

* failed authentication attempts;
* suspicious access patterns;
* unauthorized access attempts;
* security configuration changes.

A Security Event is not necessarily a confirmed Security Incident.

---

## 9.7 Security Incident

A **Security Incident** is a confirmed or reasonably suspected event that may compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems or information.

Qmova assesses incidents based on their circumstances and applicable legal and contractual requirements.

---

# 10. Communication and Integration Concepts

## 10.1 Notification

A **Notification** is a communication generated through Qmova or a connected service in response to an operational or system event.

Notifications may relate to:

* Appointments;
* Queue status;
* Visit status;
* Check-in;
* account events;
* other supported workflows.

---

## 10.2 Integration

An **Integration** is a connection between Qmova and an external service or system.

Examples include:

* Google OAuth;
* Brevo;
* WhatsApp infrastructure;
* APIs;
* other supported services.

---

## 10.3 Customer-Owned WhatsApp

**Customer-Owned WhatsApp** refers to Qmova's architecture in which a Customer connects and controls its own WhatsApp environment for supported messaging workflows.

Under this model:

* the Customer owns the WhatsApp number;
* the Customer controls its messaging account;
* Qmova provides integration infrastructure;
* Qmova does not own the Customer's WhatsApp number.

---

## 10.4 Evolution API

**Evolution API** is the WhatsApp connectivity infrastructure used by Qmova to support Customer-connected WhatsApp workflows.

It is part of the technical messaging architecture and is not a Qmova-owned customer messaging identity.

The specific hosting and operational arrangement for Evolution API is subject to the deployment architecture used by Qmova.

---

## 10.5 Brevo

**Brevo** is a third-party communications service used by Qmova for functions such as:

* OTP delivery;
* transactional email;
* other supported email notifications.

Brevo operates independently from Qmova under its own service terms and privacy practices.

---

# 11. Data and Privacy Concepts

## 11.1 Data Controller

A **Data Controller** is an organization that determines the purposes and means of processing Personal Information.

For Customer Data, the Customer will generally act as the relevant Data Controller where it determines why and how Visitor information is processed.

Qmova may act as a Data Controller for information it processes for its own purposes, such as account administration, billing, security, and certain website operations.

---

## 11.2 Data Processor

A **Data Processor** is an organization that processes Personal Information on behalf of a Data Controller.

Qmova generally acts as a Data Processor when processing Customer Data according to the Customer's instructions and applicable contractual terms.

---

## 11.3 Subprocessor

A **Subprocessor** is a third-party service provider engaged by Qmova to process Customer Data on Qmova's behalf in connection with providing the platform.

A technology component is not automatically a Subprocessor merely because Qmova uses it.

The Qmova Subprocessor List identifies relevant external organizations that qualify as Subprocessors.

---

## 11.4 Data Retention

**Data Retention** is the period and conditions under which information is kept before deletion, anonymization, or another permitted disposition.

Retention may depend on:

* legal obligations;
* contractual requirements;
* security requirements;
* Customer configuration;
* operational needs;
* backup lifecycles.

---

## 11.5 Data Export

A **Data Export** is a mechanism through which applicable Customer or User information may be provided in a portable format.

Export availability and scope depend on Qmova functionality, account type, applicable agreements, and legal requirements.

---

## 11.6 Data Deletion

**Data Deletion** is the process of removing information from applicable active Qmova systems in accordance with the applicable deletion workflow and retention requirements.

Deleted information may remain temporarily in backups until the relevant backup lifecycle expires.

---

## 11.7 Data Residency

**Data Residency** refers to the geographic location or jurisdiction in which information is stored or processed.

Because Qmova uses cloud and third-party infrastructure, processing may occur in multiple jurisdictions depending on the applicable architecture and providers.

---

## 11.8 Cookie

A **Cookie** is a small data element stored by a website or application in a user's browser or device.

Qmova may use Cookies for purposes such as:

* authentication;
* security;
* preferences;
* functionality;
* analytics.

---

## 11.9 Consent

**Consent** is a freely given, specific, informed, and appropriately recorded indication that an individual agrees to a specified processing activity where consent is the applicable legal basis.

Consent requirements differ by jurisdiction and processing context.

---

# 12. Platform and Technical Concepts

## 12.1 API

An **API (Application Programming Interface)** is a programmatic interface through which software can communicate with Qmova or supported services.

The Qmova API may be used for supported integrations, automation, and application workflows.

---

## 12.2 Webhook

A **Webhook** is a mechanism through which Qmova or another service sends an event notification to a configured endpoint.

Webhooks can be used to notify external systems when supported events occur.

---

## 12.3 Redis

**Redis** is a data-store technology used by Qmova for selected transient, operational, caching, coordination, and real-time workloads.

Redis is not necessarily the authoritative long-term source for all Qmova data.

---

## 12.4 PostgreSQL

**PostgreSQL** is the relational database technology used by Qmova for persistent application data.

---

## 12.5 Prisma

**Prisma** is the database access and ORM technology used by Qmova's backend to interact with PostgreSQL.

---

## 12.6 Socket.IO

**Socket.IO** is the real-time communication technology used by Qmova for selected live application updates.

Examples can include:

* Queue changes;
* Visit status updates;
* real-time dashboards.

---

## 12.7 Tenant Isolation

**Tenant Isolation** is the set of application, authorization, and infrastructure mechanisms intended to separate one Customer's Workspace data and access from another Customer's Workspace.

Tenant Isolation is a core requirement of Qmova's multi-tenant architecture.

# 13. Documentation Conventions

## 13.1 Customer vs. Visitor

Use **Customer** for the organization purchasing or operating Qmova.

Use **Visitor** for the individual receiving or requesting a service through the Customer.

Do not use "customer" interchangeably for both concepts in formal Qmova documentation.

---

## 13.2 Workspace vs. Tenant

Use **Workspace** in customer-facing product documentation.

Use **Tenant** when discussing the technical multi-tenant isolation model.

Avoid using "Tenant" as the primary user-interface term unless the context is technical or legal.

---

## 13.3 Appointment vs. Visit

An **Appointment** is a scheduled interaction.

A **Visit** is the broader operational record.

An Appointment can therefore result in or represent a Visit, but the terms should not automatically be treated as synonyms.

---

## 13.4 Walk-in vs. Visit

A **Walk-in** describes how a Visit originated.

A **Visit** describes the resulting operational record.

A Walk-in is therefore a type or origin of Visit rather than a replacement term for Visit.

---

## 13.5 Queue vs. Visit

A **Queue** is the operational ordering mechanism.

A **Visit** is the individual interaction moving through that mechanism.

Do not refer to a Queue and a Visit as the same object.

---

## 13.6 Product Capitalization

In formal Qmova documentation, capitalize defined product terms when they refer to the Qmova concept.

Preferred:

> The Visitor joined a Queue and their Visit moved to the next stage of the Visit Lifecycle.

Avoid:

> The customer joined a queue and their visit moved to the next stage.

The second form may be acceptable in casual explanatory content, but formal product, legal, and technical documentation should use the canonical terminology.

## Canonical Vocabulary Principle

> **Qmova uses a Visit-centric vocabulary so that Appointments, Walk-ins, Queues, and other entry paths can operate within a common Visit Lifecycle. Consistent terminology makes the product easier to understand and ensures that legal, technical, and customer documentation describe the same platform.**
